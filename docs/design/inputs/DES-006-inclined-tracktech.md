## Recommendation

Carry C as a targeted alternative to A: replace only the ends of its strip
barrels with inclined rows, retaining the same radial station identities,
central barrels, pixel system and disks. Aim to reduce the oblique material
path and ideal sensor-area requirement where a cylindrical barrel is least
efficient. Keep A and B as unchanged comparison controls.

The first engineering preference is the strixel portion: its area opportunity
is larger in this study. Inclined double-sided long strips remain in C to
expose their smaller benefit and additional engineering burden explicitly.
Do not select either inclined subsystem for construction until finite modules,
both stereo faces, supports and services demonstrate a net benefit.

## Public precedent and its limits

**FACT — TTC-F01:** `SRC-CMS-OUTER-TRACKER-2018`, Alexander Dierlamm for the
CMS Collaboration, *The CMS Outer Tracker for HL-LHC*, CMS-CR-2018-014,
2018-01-26 version, §2 / Figure 2, physical PDF page 3, describes a flat barrel
centre and tilted end sections, with module-count, material and cost motivations.
Section 5.3 on PDF page 5 and Figure 12 on PDF page 6 identify the associated
ring mechanics and cooling interfaces. See the
[public report](https://cds.cern.ch/record/2302952/files/CR2018_014.pdf) and
[source catalogue](../../../reference/manifest.yaml).

This is an engineered precedent for orientation, not evidence that nODD's
specific layout is buildable. CMS pixel-strip trigger modules differ from the
strixel and stereo assumptions here. No CMS dimensions, module inventory,
percentage saving, operating temperature or trigger function is adopted.

## Concrete geometric handoff

The following are **NODD DESIGN CHOICE — TTC-C01, proposed**, with no human
numerical approval. All lengths are in metres unless stated otherwise.

| Component | C prescription | Reason and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pixels and all disks | Retain A exactly | Isolate inclination from B's additional forward pixels. C does not address A's forward-pixel weakness. |
| Strip station radii | Strixels: 0.260, 0.360, 0.500, 0.660; long-strip pair references: 0.820, 1.020 | Retain A's radial lever-arm controls and parent station identities. |
| Central strip barrels | Keep the cylindrical section `-0.600 <= z <= 0.600` at each radius | Concentrate the first inclination test in the more oblique end sections. |
| Positive-end row centres | `z_c = 0.650, 0.750, 0.850, 0.950, 1.050, 1.150`, each at its parent radius `r_0` | Six reference rows span the former end section. These are envelope rows, not a selected module length or count. |
| Inclination | `alpha = min(atan(z_c/r_0), pi/4)` | Aim the surface normal towards the origin, limiting tilt to 45 degrees to bound radial excursion and mechanical complexity. |
| Negative ends | Reflect each positive row through `z=0` | Preserve the declared reflection symmetry; the axial component of the surface normal changes sign. |
| Row edge allowance | Add 0.010 along the surface tangent at each edge; compare 0 and 0.005 controls | Protect the sampled shifted-vertex coverage at a visible overlap/material cost. This is not a fabrication clearance. |

The inherited active host remains `0.025 <= r <= 1.140`, `abs(z) <= 3.150`.
The shared external service reservation is not spent on C. The change requires
new tilted supports and routing hypotheses within the existing host, not a
claim that A's unqualified support arrangement can simply be rotated.

**INFERENCE — TTC-I01, geometry derived from TTC-C01:** each positive row is
the finite conical envelope generated by rotating the straight segment

```text
r + tan(alpha) * (z - z_c) = r_0
```

about the beam axis. Its meridional normal is
`(cos(alpha), sin(alpha))` in `(r,z)`. A real row would contain planar modules
at discrete azimuths; the cone is an axisymmetric screening approximation.
It preserves the chosen meridional tilt and normal material allowance, while
omitting azimuthal edges, module corners, stagger, support pieces and services.
It is not a continuous conical silicon sensor or a construction drawing.

The unextended endpoints match the *origin angular coverage* of A's reference
interval `z_b = z_c +/- 0.050` on the cylinder:

```text
t = tan(alpha)
r_edge = (r_0 + t*z_c) / (1 + t*z_b/r_0)
z_edge = z_b*r_edge/r_0
u_edge = (z_edge - z_c)/cos(alpha)
```

Use `u_min - delta` and `u_max + delta`, with nominal `delta=0.010`, then
`r(u)=r_0-u*sin(alpha)` and `z(u)=z_c+u*cos(alpha)`. Matching origin angular
boundaries without extension does not preserve shifted-vertex coverage.
The 0/5/10 mm variants expose that trade-off rather than concealing a gap.
All finite endpoints must be checked against the host and other component
envelopes. Passing an ideal-surface check is not passing a physical overlap test.

## Double-sided long strips: material and information accounting

**NODD DESIGN CHOICE — TTC-C02, explicit inherited module hypothesis:** a
long-strip module has **two silicon sensor faces**, one scalar strip
measurement per face, with symmetric `+/-20 mrad` stereo measurement axes and
20 micrometre independent scalar uncertainty. The faces form one local
double-sided stereo assembly. This statement does not select a double-sided
implant process on one wafer; physical sensor separation, readout, thicknesses
and common support remain to be designed. C inclines the entire assembly,
preserving its relative stereo angle.

**INFERENCE — TTC-I02:** for two surviving, independently measured faces the
collapsed station has
`sigma_fine = 20/(sqrt(2)*cos(0.020))` micrometres and
`sigma_coarse = 20/(sqrt(2)*sin(0.020))` micrometres, inherited from DES-006.
Those are two scalar coordinates of one stereo station, not two independent
space points. The illustrative **2% X0 normal allowance belongs to the entire
pair once**, not 2% per face. Its constituent division is unknown; a bookkeeping
split must not be presented as a measured 1% per sensor budget.

Keep these observables separate:

| Quantity | Counting rule |
| --- | --- |
| Distinct radial measurement stations | Count one parent long-strip station even if a trajectory crosses overlapping rows of that station. |
| Traversed physical row assemblies | Count every crossed row, including overlap rows; these carry real material in the fixture. |
| Long-strip scalar face measurements | Two per crossed ideal double-sided assembly, subject to the unresolved face masks, pairing and losses. |
| Long-strip sensor area proxy | Twice the paired reference surface area, before inactive edges, tiling and overlaps beyond the explicitly represented rows. |
| Directional material | Apply the pair allowance once to each assembly crossed, with its actual incidence; never deduplicate material by parent station. |

An overlap hit is not free material and two sides are not two independent
radial lever arms. Loss of one side invalidates the collapsed two-coordinate
response. Inclination also changes the mapping of the coarse coordinate into
global `r/z`; an unmodified cylindrical covariance cannot establish C's
longitudinal performance.

## Cost-benefit assessment

**INFERENCE — TTC-I03:** an outward straight ray from the origin has
meridional unit direction `(1/cosh(eta), tanh(eta))`. For a positive tilted row,
the thin-layer path factor is
`cosh(eta)/abs(cos(alpha)+sinh(eta)*sin(alpha))`, versus `cosh(eta)` for a
cylinder. This explains the potential saving with unchanged normal material.
It applies per crossing; overlapping rows, curvature, shifted vertices and
passive material require the explicit scan. No percentage reduction in a
complete material budget follows from this formula alone.

**INFERENCE — TTC-I04:** integrating ideal surface area gives
`2*pi*r_0*central_length` for a retained cylinder and
`pi*(r_start+r_end)*(u_max-u_min)` for one conical row. Summing the specified
rows and both ends gives the following *barrel-only* proxies; disks are
unchanged. Values were calculated from TTC-C01, not from a component inventory.

| Strip barrel reference area [m²] | A | C, no extension | C, 5 mm each edge | C, 10 mm each edge |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Strixels | 26.84177 | 20.13929 | 21.48325 | 22.82722 |
| Long-strip pair reference | 27.74655 | 23.87222 | 25.26048 | 26.64873 |

At the nominal extension, the ideal strixel barrel area is about 15.0% smaller;
the paired long-strip barrel area is only about 4.0% smaller. Actual two-face
long-strip sensor area doubles both entries, preserving that percentage.
These are area opportunities, not established reductions in installed module
count, money, cooling load or total detector mass. Continuous row lengths are
not automatically compatible with reusable module outlines; rounding to real
tiles may consume much of the saving.

| Benefit to test | Associated cost or failure mode | Decision evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Less oblique material through inclined sensor assemblies | Overlap trajectories cross additional complete assemblies; local material spikes may exceed A | Directional scans that count every crossing and retain maxima and transition details, including 0/5/10 mm variants |
| Less ideal sensor area for comparable angular coverage | Real module dimensions, guard rings and azimuthal overlaps may erase the proxy saving | A/C tiled layouts using identical qualified module types and explicit two-face silicon inventories |
| Possible lower readout and power burden if fewer modules suffice | More ring mounts, interconnect turns and cooling interfaces; tilted routing may add passive material | Module counts, channels, power, coolant, flex and support BOM by subsystem |
| Same nominal station sequence and retained outer radii | Different actual radii, coarse-coordinate projection, stereo pairing and alignment correlations | Full local measurement covariance, module-aware propagation, one-face and one-row loss studies |
| More realistic orientation than a long flat end barrel | Greater assembly, survey, removal and thermal-contact complexity | Prototype support/routing concept, corner clearances and tolerance/thermal assessment |

TrackTech therefore supports C as a material/coverage investigation, with a
conditional preference to pursue tilted strixels first if the nominal screen
supports it. The small long-strip area margin makes its inclusion harder to
justify once two-face assemblies and mechanics are costed. No new low-material
sensor technology, thinner support or improved intrinsic resolution is assumed.

## Handoff and remaining review

PhysVal should compare A/C with identical field, vertex, response and normal
material scenarios; report station loss, duplicate row crossings, material
increases as well as reductions, and unchanged forward-pixel limitations.
The [independent inclined assessment](DES-006-inclined-physval.md) carries that
judgement. Existing A/B IdRes results remain A/B evidence: a cone-to-cylinder
substitution must not be labelled a validated C resolution calculation.

Before a production preference, TrackTech and SysArch must supply planar module
outlines, both long-strip face transforms, masks, supports, thermal interfaces
and routed services. PhysVal must evaluate the resulting occupancy, ambiguity,
misalignment, efficiency and momentum/vertexing trade-offs. Human review retains
the decision; this contribution supplies a bounded alternative and its costs.
